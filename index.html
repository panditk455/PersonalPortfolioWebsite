Carls Swap

Carls Swap Project

Team Members: Kritika Pandit, Owen Xu, Lucky Beulla Muhoza, Daniel Scheider

Project Overview: Along with my colleagues, I am working on this project, Carls Swap, which addresses real-life problems faced by students and staff at Carleton College. Currently, there isn’t an efficient system for Carleton students to exchange items during the school year or for the end-of-year cleanup. Our project aims to provide an interface for students to offer extra items they no longer need to other students during the term. Additionally, it will offer a better system for the Center for Community and Civic Engagement (CCCE) office to acquire and sell items collected during the end-of-term cleanup. Students will also be able to reserve certain items acquired by CCCE to ensure that valuable items aren’t sold to random people during summer breaks. We are in the process of interviewing people and preparing axial codings to design a prototype based on user needs.

Research & UX Process

Our research involved semi-structured interviews and fictional inquiries with six Carleton members, including CCCE workers and experienced students. During the interviews, participants provided sketches of their ideal system, which played a crucial role in shaping our initial prototype. Following the interviews, we performed axial coding to identify core themes that emerged from the data. This helped us pinpoint key insights, which we used to guide the development of the app. We then conducted an iterative design process, incorporating feedback from students to refine and improve the prototype.

Prototype & Design Process

After gathering insights from the interviews, I developed the initial Carls Swap prototype. Participants expressed a preference for an app due to its convenience, as they wouldn't always have access to a computer. They highlighted important features, including the ability to buy and sell items, upload item photos, and share Venmo details for transactions. They also emphasized the need for item categories, filters, the ability to reserve items, and a search feature.

Based on these insights, I created a low-fidelity prototype in Figma. After receiving feedback from classmates, I made several adjustments, such as revising a misleading clickable banner, improving color choices for accessibility, and updating the messaging system to allow price negotiations, similar to platforms like eBay.

A key feature I added was the "reserve" option, which allows users to hold an item for a set period, after which it is returned to the community if not purchased. We also implemented a system to handle canceled transactions and banned users, ensuring that problematic sellers are removed and cannot return to the platform. The system tracks Carleton student IDs to maintain security.

View the Figma Prototype for detailed interaction flows and View the Axial Coding for our axial coding process.

Three Main App Requirements

  • Exchanging items without storage: Students can coordinate meetups using the messaging feature to exchange items, eliminating the need for storage space.
  • Wishlist and notifications: Users receive notifications when an item they were looking for becomes available, saving them from constant manual searches.
  • Item tracking for CCCE inventory: Helps CCCE track and manage inventory, ensuring high-demand items are saved for New Student Week sales and not sold over the summer.

    • How the prototype fulfills the requirements:

    The prototype includes a messaging feature that allows students to coordinate meetups for item exchanges. This also facilitates negotiations, enabling users to request discounts or discuss item details. Additionally, the prototype offers a Wishlist and Notifications feature. Users can save items they like by creating a cart. If a user searches for an item that is unavailable, they will automatically receive a notification when someone adds it to the platform. This saves time by eliminating the need for constant manual checks. When a user adds an item to their cart, it is temporarily removed from public listings to prevent others from purchasing it. However, if the item is not bought within two days, it is automatically returned to the available listings. Once purchased, the seller can delete the listing or update details such as price and availability. This system ensures real-time tracking of sold items, keeping the lisitings updated. There is a history section where the previously sold items are tracked. CCCE will be the main admin where they can access all the items sold in the past by other users, accumulated together as a list, ensuring that the high demand items are kept for new Student Week when they get something.
